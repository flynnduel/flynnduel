# MAAN Calculation Guide

> Metodología desarrollada por Club de Negociación (clubdenegociacion.com) — Consultoría Francisco Gutiérrez

## What Is MAAN?

**MAAN** (Mejor Alternativa a un Acuerdo Negociado) — known in English as BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) — is the single most important concept in negotiation. It is your best course of action if the current negotiation fails completely and you walk away.

Your MAAN determines your real negotiating power. A strong MAAN means you can walk away confidently. A weak MAAN means you're dependent on this deal — and the other side may sense it.

### The Critical Distinction

**MAAN is EXOGENOUS — it exists OUTSIDE the current negotiation.**

It is NOT:
- What the counterparty is offering
- Your desired outcome
- Your bottom line (that's your reservation price, derived FROM your MAAN)
- A bluff or threat

It IS:
- What you would actually DO if there's no deal
- Your best option that doesn't involve the counterparty
- Something that exists independently of this negotiation

## Correct vs Incorrect MAAN Examples

### Example 1: Salary Negotiation

**Situation**: You're negotiating salary for a new job at Company A. They offered $110,000.

| | Example | Correct? |
|---|---------|----------|
| INCORRECT | "My MAAN is to ask them for $120,000" | No — that's still negotiating with Company A |
| INCORRECT | "My MAAN is their minimum offer of $110,000" | No — that's the counterparty's position |
| INCORRECT | "My MAAN is to threaten to leave" | No — threats are not alternatives |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is the offer I have from Company B for $105,000" | Yes — independent of Company A |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is to stay at my current job making $100,000" | Yes — exists outside this negotiation |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is to freelance; based on my pipeline, I'd earn ~$95,000" | Yes — independent alternative |

**Best MAAN**: The Company B offer at $105,000 (highest value alternative).

**Reservation price**: Any offer above $105,000 from Company A is better than walking away.

### Example 2: Vendor Contract

**Situation**: You're negotiating a software license with Vendor X at $50,000/year.

| | Example | Correct? |
|---|---------|----------|
| INCORRECT | "My MAAN is to negotiate a discount with Vendor X" | No — still within this negotiation |
| INCORRECT | "My MAAN is to tell them I'll leave" | No — that's a tactic, not an alternative |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is Vendor Y's quote at $45,000/year with fewer features" | Yes — independent option |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is to build the functionality in-house (est. $120,000 one-time)" | Yes — independent option |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is to continue using our current manual process (costing us ~$30,000/year in labor)" | Yes — status quo is an alternative |

**Best MAAN**: Vendor Y at $45,000 (weighing features and switching costs).

### Example 3: Rent Negotiation

**Situation**: Your lease is expiring and the landlord wants to raise rent from $2,000 to $2,400/month.

| | Example | Correct? |
|---|---------|----------|
| INCORRECT | "My MAAN is to refuse the increase" | No — that's a position, not an alternative |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is to move to the apartment on 5th Street at $2,100/month" | Yes — independent alternative |
| CORRECT | "My MAAN is to move in with my partner and split their $1,800 rent" | Yes — exists outside this negotiation |

## Step-by-Step MAAN Identification Process

### Step 1: List ALL Alternatives

Write down every possible option if this negotiation produces no agreement. Include:
- Other deals you could pursue
- The status quo (doing nothing / keeping current situation)
- Creating something new (build vs buy, DIY, etc.)
- Waiting (negotiating later with more information)
- Combining partial alternatives

**Don't filter yet** — list everything, even bad options.

### Step 2: Evaluate Each Alternative

For each alternative, assess:

| Criterion | How to Evaluate |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Financial value** | What's the monetary outcome? |
| **Non-financial value** | Flexibility, relationship, learning, reputation? |
| **Feasibility** | Can you actually execute this? Realistically? |
| **Timeline** | How quickly can you implement it? |
| **Risk** | What could go wrong? What's the downside? |
| **Cost of switching** | What does it cost to pursue this alternative? |

### Step 3: Identify Your Best Alternative

Select the **single best** alternative considering all criteria above. This is your MAAN.

If two alternatives are close in value, note both but pick one as your primary MAAN for reservation price calculation.

### Step 4: Calculate Your Reservation Price

Your reservation price (precio de reserva) is the **worst deal you should accept** in the current negotiation. It's the point of indifference between accepting the deal and walking to your MAAN.

**Formula**:
```
Reservation Price = MAAN Value + Switching Costs − MAAN Risks
```

**Example**:
- MAAN: Job offer from Company B at $105,000
- Switching cost: Company B is in a less desirable city (worth -$5,000 to you)
- MAAN risk: Company B is a startup, less stable (worth -$3,000 in risk)
- Reservation price with Company A: $105,000 - $5,000 - $3,000 = **$97,000**
- Any offer from Company A above $97,000 is better than your MAAN

### Step 5: Assess Their MAAN

Estimate the counterparty's MAAN using the same process:
- What are their alternatives if there's no deal with you?
- How good are those alternatives?
- What's their likely reservation price?

**If their MAAN is weak** (few alternatives, high switching costs), you have more leverage.
**If their MAAN is strong** (many good alternatives), you may need to create more value.

## MAAN Improvement Strategies

Don't just identify your MAAN — actively improve it before negotiating:

### 1. Generate More Alternatives
- Get competing offers or quotes
- Research market alternatives you haven't considered
- Create new options (partnerships, DIY solutions, timing changes)

### 2. Strengthen Existing Alternatives
- Advance conversations with alternative parties
- Get written offers (a verbal possibility is weaker than a signed letter)
- Reduce switching costs to your best alternative

### 3. Worsen Their MAAN (Ethically)
- Demonstrate your unique value that alternatives can't provide
- Build relationships that make switching costly for them
- Time negotiations when their alternatives are limited

### 4. Buy Time
- If your MAAN is weak NOW but will improve, delay the negotiation
- "I need a few weeks to consider" can create space to develop alternatives
- Never negotiate under artificial urgency when your MAAN is weak

## When to Reveal Your MAAN

### Reveal When:
- Your MAAN is genuinely strong and you want to signal power
- The counterparty underestimates your alternatives
- You want to accelerate a stalled negotiation
- Transparency builds trust in a collaborative relationship

### Protect When:
- Your MAAN is weak (revealing it gives them leverage)
- You're unsure about the other side's intentions
- The negotiation is early stage (gather information first)
- Revealing would damage the relationship ("I have other offers" can feel like a threat)

### Never:
- Fabricate a MAAN you don't have
- Bluff about an alternative that doesn't exist
- Use MAAN as a threat rather than information

## Common MAAN Mistakes

### 1. Having No MAAN
Walking into a negotiation with no alternative means you MUST accept whatever is offered. Always develop at least one alternative before negotiating.

### 2. Confusing Wishes with MAAN
"I hope I get another offer" is not a MAAN. A MAAN must be concrete and executable. Can you actually walk away and do this? If not, it's a wish.

### 3. Overestimating Your MAAN
People tend to overvalue their alternatives. Be honest: if your MAAN is a job offer for less money in a worse location, don't pretend it's equivalent.

### 4. Ignoring Non-Financial Factors
A MAAN isn't just about money. Consider: work-life balance, career growth, relationship quality, risk, timeline, personal satisfaction.

### 5. Revealing Weakness
If your MAAN is weak, don't share it. Focus the conversation on interests and creative options instead of walkaway power.

### 6. Failing to Improve It
Your MAAN isn't fixed. Before a critical negotiation, invest time in developing better alternatives. Even small improvements to your MAAN shift the power dynamic.

### 7. Treating the Counterparty's Offer as Your MAAN
This is the #1 mistake. "Well, they offered X, so my alternative is X." No — X is their proposal. Your MAAN is what happens OUTSIDE this negotiation.

## MAAN in Different Contexts

### Salary Negotiation
- MAAN: Other job offers, staying in current role, freelancing
- Reservation price: Minimum salary that's better than alternatives
- Tip: Get at least one competing offer before negotiating

### Vendor/Supplier
- MAAN: Alternative vendors, build in-house, status quo
- Reservation price: Maximum price below alternative + switching costs
- Tip: Always get 3 quotes minimum

### Partnership/Joint Venture
- MAAN: Go alone, find a different partner, don't pursue the opportunity
- Reservation price: Terms where partnership value exceeds going alone
- Tip: Model the "go alone" scenario in detail

### Internal (within organization)
- MAAN: Do it yourself, find another sponsor, escalate, wait
- Reservation price: Minimum support/resources that make the project viable
- Tip: Internal MAANs are often weaker — focus on shared interests

### Real Estate
- MAAN: Other properties, rent vs buy, wait for market changes
- Reservation price: Maximum price below MAAN value + preference premium
- Tip: Always have a backup property identified

---

*Your MAAN is your source of power. Know it, improve it, and use it wisely.*
